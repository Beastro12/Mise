# Learner profile — Pietro

AI Solutions business developer at CGI Finland. Sells Agent Harbor (a managed
operational layer for AI agents) and Application Factory. He is learning the
techniques in depth, well past what the sales role needs, because he wants to
be able to tell when a claim about agents is true.

Not an engineer. Builds real things with Claude and Claude Code: Project PG,
an educational video generator, Silta, an AI news digest.

## How he thinks

- Studied education. Learns through analogy and systems thinking, and will
  reach for a model from another domain (coaching, military command,
  journalism) before reaching for the technical one. Those analogies are
  usually load-bearing, not decorative — engage with them, then name the
  established concept and say where the analogy stops holding.
- Strong epistemic discipline. He distinguishes what he knows from what he
  assumes, and he expects the same back. Unmarked speculation loses his trust
  faster than being told something is unverified.
- Weaker engineering hygiene: testing, version control, reproducibility,
  naming. Worth reinforcing when it comes up naturally in a build, not as a
  lecture.
- Breadth over depth by default — many parallel projects. The app exists to
  force depth, so holding him to one concept per session is doing him a
  favour, not being obstinate.

## What works

Challenge and diagnosis. Tell him what is wrong with his version and why.
Padding, encouragement and restatement of his own point back to him all waste
the session.

## Known pattern

He answers at gist level and stops: names the method, not the mechanism. The
second sentence is where the learning is. Ask for it every time.

## Placement result (2026-10-24)

Strong on failure-mode reasoning — correlated errors, silent telemetry,
Goodhart. Partial on mechanics: he reached for a "knowledge base" rather than
weights, and attributed output variation to context rather than sampling.
Partial on context degradation and on eval method. Weak on the workflow versus
agent distinction, and on the EU AI Act, which he answered with a GDPR and
data-residency frame.
